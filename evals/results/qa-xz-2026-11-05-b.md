## The change

One edit in `codebase_qa_service.py`, in two parts, both on the writer's
side of the one LLM call. Motivation, from e's attribution: four of the
five lost points were facts sitting in the evidence that the writer did
not draw, and `_build_context` was labelling every chain body
`relevance: 1.0` — the writer had Jev's ranking for the seeds (knn
scores) and nothing for the chains.

1. **Jev's numbers on the chain bodies.** `_jev_expand` no longer appends
   chain bodies with `score: 1.0`; `score` is the accept probability of
   the verdict that admitted the body, and a `jev` dict carries
   `accept`, `final` (the second look's accept, when there was one),
   `verdict` and `how` (the `why` trail). The UI's sources list shows
   these probabilities for chain bodies, which is why this run's sources
   lists have two bands — seeds at 2.0–3.0 (knn score) and chains at
   0.0–1.0 (Jev accept) — where e's lists had every chain body at 1.000.
2. **Grouping and an index.** `_build_context` first orders the bodies
   with `_order_for_writer`: grouped by file, files ordered by their best
   body, and within a file whole-file bodies first, then seeds by knn
   score, then chain bodies by accept probability. When there is more
   than one body, the context opens with `_evidence_index`: one line per
   body — number, file, symbol, label, how it was reached — under a
   heading that says how many bodies and that they are grouped by file.
   Each body's header carries the label from `_jev_label`: "Jev score: X
   of 4" for seeds, "Jev accept: p, final look q, verdict" for chain
   bodies.

Nothing about Jev's calls, thresholds or budgets changed; the Jev cost
differences in the table are retrieval variance (the knn set and the
probabilities both move between runs, see e's "Between d and e").
